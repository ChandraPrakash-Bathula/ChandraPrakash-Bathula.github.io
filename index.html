About

Research interests grounded in experimentation and implementation.

I am an AI/ML practitioner and Adjunct Faculty member at Saint Louis University. My work spans computer vision, deep learning, applied machine learning, and AI-enabled software systems.

My computer vision research includes controlled comparisons between convolutional networks and vision transformers, as well as experiments combining pretrained visual backbones with convolutional and deformable feature-processing modules. I am particularly interested in separating architectural effects from initialization, pretraining, data scale, and experimental design.

Alongside research, I develop interactive educational tools and reproducible demonstrations that make modern machine learning methods easier to inspect, test, and understand. I also teach software development, analytics, and AI-related topics.

Current research directions
  • CNNs, vision transformers, and visual representations
  • Controlled evaluation and reproducible deep learning
  • Image segmentation and convolution-attention hybrids
  • Efficient model design and experimental analysis
  • Multimodal learning and AI systems

Selected Projects

Research experiments, model implementations, and interactive demonstrations.

P01

Controlled CNN–Vision Transformer Benchmark

A systematic benchmark designed to study architecture and initialization separately. The experimental grid covers 10 architectures, 9 datasets, 3 training-data fractions, and 2 random seeds, totaling 540 architecture–dataset–fraction– seed configurations before any additional model variants or repeated runs. The work investigates how scratch training and pretrained initialization influence comparative conclusions.

Computer Vision CNN ViT Benchmarking PyTorch
GitHub ↗
P02

Expert-Branch Convolutional Modules for Visual Segmentation

Experiments combining pretrained vision transformers with convolutional expert branches, including standard, deformable, and depthwise-separable convolutional operators. Evaluated on CAMO, COD10K, and DSAD to study whether convolutional feature processing improves segmentation performance over a pretrained visual backbone.

Image Segmentation Deformable Convolution Vision Transformers PyTorch
